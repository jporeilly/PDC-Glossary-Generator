4. AI ASSISTANCE - NATURE, LIMITS AND OVERSIGHT

The software uses an artificial intelligence system to draft glossary
definitions and to suggest wording. It runs against a local model
(Ollama) or a hosted provider you configure.

  (a) AI interaction. Content marked as AI-drafted is generated by an
      AI system, not written by a person.

  (b) Outputs are probabilistic. Outputs are generated by a
      statistical language model and are inherently probabilistic.
      They may be factually incorrect, fabricated, out of date,
      incomplete, misleading or biased ("hallucinations"), may not
      reflect your data, and may differ between runs for the same
      input. Outputs are AI-generated content and should be treated
      as such.

  (c) No professional advice. Outputs are provided for informational
      purposes only and do not constitute professional advice of any
      kind, including legal, financial, regulatory or data protection
      advice.

  (d) Human oversight. Every AI-drafted term is presented for review
      before it is applied, and you must review it. A glossary
      published to a catalog becomes the organisation's stated
      description of its data; do not publish AI-drafted wording that
      no one has read. You remain responsible for what you publish.

  (e) Classification is NOT decided by AI. Personal-data detection,
      category assignment and sensitivity levels are determined by
      deterministic rules from the profiled scan and the domain pack,
      not by the language model, and the software re-asserts them
      after any AI pass. This is deliberate: a probabilistic
      classifier deciding what counts as personal data would place
      the resulting system in a materially different regulatory
      position. Do not modify the software to let AI set those
      fields.

  (f) Prompts and what is sent. Prompts include table and column
      names, and - where profiling is enabled - sample values and
      induced value patterns from your data. Where you configure a
      HOSTED provider (Anthropic, OpenAI, Azure OpenAI or Google),
      that content leaves your machine and is processed under that
      provider's terms. Where you use a local model, it does not. Do
      not enable a hosted provider for data you may not disclose to a
      third party.

  (g) Output rights. No representation is made as to the ownership,
      originality or non-infringement of any Output. You are
      responsible for checking that your use of an Output does not
      infringe the rights of any third party. No indemnity is given
      in respect of Outputs.

  (h) Model components. Any AI model used is supplied by a third
      party under its own licence and use policy, which you are
      responsible for complying with. No warranty is given in respect
      of any such model.


5. DATA PROTECTION AND PRIVACY

  (a) Data kept on your machine. Connections, settings, scan results,
      saved glossaries, the governed dictionary and the audit log are
      stored locally in the per-user data directory. The software
      transmits none of it except as described in (b) and (c).

  (b) Your catalog. Applying a glossary sends the terms, categories,
      tags and quality expectations you have approved to the Pentaho
      Data Catalog instance you configure. Nothing is sent until you
      choose to apply.

  (c) Hosted AI providers. See section 4(f). This is the only route
      by which the contents of your data can leave the machine, it is
      off unless you turn it on, and a local model avoids it
      entirely.

  (d) Credentials. Data source credentials are stored in the per-user
      data directory so that scans can be repeated. AI provider API
      keys are deliberately NOT written to the settings file, so that
      exporting or sharing application state does not disclose them.
      Protect the per-user data directory accordingly.

  (e) Personal data in your estate. A scan may identify columns
      holding personal data and record their names and profiled
      characteristics. Where profiling is enabled, sample values may
      include personal data. Enable profiling only where you are
      entitled to process that data for this purpose, and prefer a
      non-production copy where one exists.

  (f) Controller. The organisation operating the software determines
      the purposes and means of this processing and is the data
      controller. It is responsible for its own privacy notice,
      lawful basis, retention periods, transfer mechanisms and
      processor terms, including in respect of any hosted AI provider
      it chooses to enable. The authors of the software supply a tool
      and operate no service on your behalf.

  (g) Your rights. Subject to applicable law you have the right to
      request access to, rectification of, or erasure of your
      personal data, to restrict or object to its processing, and to
      data portability, and to complain to your data protection
      supervisory authority. Exercise these rights with the

  (h) Data minimisation. Scan only the sources you need, and disable
